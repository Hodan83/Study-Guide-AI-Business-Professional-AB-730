## Section 1: AI Fundamentals

### Question 1
**What is the primary difference between supervised and unsupervised learning?**

A) Supervised learning is faster than unsupervised learning  
B) Supervised learning uses labeled data, while unsupervised learning finds patterns in unlabeled data  
C) Unsupervised learning is more accurate than supervised learning  
D) Supervised learning can only be used for classification tasks  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Supervised learning requires labeled training data where the correct outputs are known, allowing the model to learn the relationship between inputs and outputs. Unsupervised learning works with unlabeled data to discover hidden patterns, structures, or groupings without predefined categories.
</details>

### Question 2
**Which of the following best describes Deep Learning?**

A) A type of database management system  
B) A subset of machine learning using neural networks with multiple layers  
C) A programming language for AI  
D) A method for storing large amounts of data  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Deep Learning is a subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with multiple hidden layers (hence "deep") to learn hierarchical representations of data. It's particularly effective for complex tasks like image recognition and natural language processing.
</details>

### Question 3
**What is Natural Language Processing (NLP) primarily used for?**

A) Processing numerical data  
B) Understanding and generating human language  
C) Creating computer graphics  
D) Managing databases  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of AI that focuses on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language. Applications include chatbots, translation services, sentiment analysis, and text summarization.
</details>

### Question 4
**Which statement about current AI technology is most accurate?**

A) AI has achieved human-level general intelligence  
B) Most AI systems today are narrow AI designed for specific tasks  
C) AI systems can perform any task without training data  
D) AI systems are completely unbiased and objective  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Current AI technology consists primarily of narrow (or weak) AI systems designed to excel at specific tasks. We have not yet achieved artificial general intelligence (AGI) that can perform any intellectual task like a human. AI systems require training data and can inherit biases from that data.
</details>

## Section 2: Business Applications

### Question 5
**Which AI application would be most appropriate for reducing customer service costs while maintaining 24/7 availability?**

A) Predictive maintenance  
B) Chatbots and virtual assistants  
C) Fraud detection systems  
D) Supply chain optimization  

<details>

B) Natural language processing

## Section 3: AI Implementation

### Question 9
**What is the recommended approach when starting an AI implementation?**

A) Deploy the most complex model available  
B) Start with a pilot project and prove value before scaling  
C) Automate all business processes simultaneously  
D) Skip testing and deploy directly to production  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Best practice is to start with a pilot project that has clear objectives and measurable outcomes. Prove value on a small scale, learn from the experience, and then scale gradually. This minimizes risk and allows for iteration and improvement.
</details>

### Question 10
**In the AI project lifecycle, what should happen BEFORE model development?**

A) Deployment to production  
B) User training  
C) Data collection and preparation  
D) ROI celebration  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Data collection and preparation must occur before model development. You need quality training data to build effective AI models. The typical lifecycle is: problem definition → data collection/preparation → model development → testing → deployment → monitoring.
</details>

### Question 11
**What is "model drift" and why is it important?**

A) Physical movement of servers in a data center  
B) Degradation of model performance over time as data patterns change  
C) The cost increase of running AI models  
D) The process of training new models  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Model drift occurs when the statistical properties of the data change over time, causing the model's predictions to become less accurate. Regular monitoring and retraining are essential to maintain model performance in production environments.
</details>

### Question 12
**Which metric would be most appropriate for evaluating a binary classification model (e.g., fraud detection)?**

A) Mean Squared Error (MSE)  
B) Silhouette Score  
C) Precision and Recall  
D) Perplexity  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: C**

Explanation: For binary classification tasks, Precision (how many predicted positives are actually positive) and Recall (how many actual positives were correctly identified) are key metrics. For fraud detection, you need to balance catching fraud (recall) with minimizing false alarms (precision).
</details>

## Section 4: Ethics and Governance

### Question 13
**Which of the following is an example of algorithmic bias?**

A) A computer running slowly  
B) A recruitment AI that systematically ranks one gender lower due to biased training data  
C) A model that requires too much computational power  
D) A system that crashes frequently  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: Algorithmic bias occurs when an AI system produces systematically prejudiced results, often due to biased training data. A recruitment AI favoring one gender over another based on historical hiring patterns is a clear example of how AI can perpetuate and amplify existing biases.
</details>

### Question 14
**What is the primary purpose of AI explainability?**

A) To make models run faster  
B) To reduce development costs  
C) To understand and explain how AI systems make decisions  
D) To eliminate the need for human oversight  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: C**

Explanation: AI explainability (also called interpretability) enables stakeholders to understand how and why an AI system makes particular decisions. This is crucial for building trust, ensuring accountability, meeting regulatory requirements, and detecting bias or errors.
</details>

### Question 15
**Which principle is NOT typically part of responsible AI frameworks?**

A) Fairness  
B) Transparency  
C) Maximizing profit at all costs  
D) Accountability  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: C**

Explanation: Responsible AI frameworks focus on ethical principles like fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, and safety. While business value is important, maximizing profit at the expense of ethical considerations is not part of responsible AI practices.
</details>

### Question 16
**Under GDPR, what right do individuals have regarding automated decision-making?**

A) No rights; AI decisions cannot be questioned  
B) Right to an explanation of the decision  
C) Right to unlimited data storage  
D) Right to free AI services  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: B**

Explanation: GDPR grants individuals the "right to explanation" for automated decisions that significantly affect them. Organizations must be able to explain how their AI systems reach decisions, which is why transparency and explainability are crucial in AI implementations.
</details>

## Section 5: Integrated Scenarios

### Question 17
**A healthcare provider wants to implement AI for patient diagnosis assistance. Which considerations are MOST important? (Select the best answer)**

A) Using the cheapest AI solution available  
B) Ensuring model accuracy, explainability, and regulatory compliance  
C) Implementing as quickly as possible without testing  
D) Keeping the system secret from patients and doctors  

<details>

### Question 18
**A company's AI system shows disparate outcomes for different demographic groups. What should be the FIRST step?**

A) Ignore the issue if overall accuracy is high  
B) Immediately shut down the system  
C) Investigate the root cause through bias auditing  
D) Increase marketing to the affected groups  

<details>
<summary>Click to see answer</summary>

**Answer: C**

Explanation: The first step should be to investigate and understand the root cause through systematic bias auditing. This involves examining the training data, model design, and deployment context. Only after understanding the issue can appropriate corrective actions be taken.
</details>

### Question 19

A) Measuring only the initial implementation costs

C) Focusing solely on technology metrics like model accuracy

## Study Tips

### For Multiple Choice Questions:
- Read the question carefully
- Eliminate obviously incorrect answers first
- Watch for absolute words like "always," "never," "all"
- Choose the MOST appropriate answer
- Consider practical business implications

### For Scenario Questions:
- Identify the key business problem
- Consider stakeholder impacts
- Think about ethical implications
- Balance technical feasibility with business value
- Consider both short-term and long-term effects

### Common Exam Topics:
- Definitions and distinctions (AI, ML, DL)
- Business applications and use cases
- Implementation best practices
- Ethical considerations and bias
- ROI and measurement
- Governance and compliance
- Data requirements and quality
